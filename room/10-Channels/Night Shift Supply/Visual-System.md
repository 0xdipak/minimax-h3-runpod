# Visual System

The `look` block in [[Brand]] is the machine-readable version of this page.
This page is the reasoning, so that when someone proposes changing a line
they can see what it was holding up.

## Why expired 16mm

The format's native look is a clean phone sensor. Grain and halation read as
*not-phone* within the first half-second of a scroll, which is the entire
budget we have to stop a thumb.

## Why one light source

Practical single-source lighting is the most reproducible instruction you can
give a generative model. Multi-source setups drift between clips; "lit only by
the cooler" comes back the same every time. Consistency here is worth more than
beauty.

## Why the red object

Sodium amber and fluorescent green are near-complements of nothing — the frame
goes muddy without an anchor. One saturated red object per composition gives the
eye a place to land, and gives the room a continuity token to plant deliberately.

## Why the camera doesn't move

Two reasons, one aesthetic and one practical. Aesthetically, the bystander
frame is the brand. Practically, a locked-off camera is the single strongest
lever on inter-clip coherence — a moving camera invents geometry, and invented
geometry does not match the clip before it.

## What a writer may specify

Framing, height, and whether the frame holds or is interrupted. Not stock, not
grade, not palette. Those are settled here and inherited.
