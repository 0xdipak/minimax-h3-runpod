# Voice and Tone

The look is enforced by the compiler. The voice is enforced by the room. This
page is the standard we hold each other to in notes.

## The register

Flat. Nobody in this world is trying to be funny, and that is what makes it
funny. A line lands because it is the plainest possible statement of something
absurd, delivered by someone too tired to be surprised by it.

## Rules for dialogue

- **Six words or fewer**, almost always. A 10-second clip carrying two people
  talking has room for about eighteen words total, including the pauses.
- **Nobody explains.** No character has ever clarified a joke in this world.
- **Answer the wrong question.** The most reliable turn in the format is a
  character responding to something adjacent to what was asked.
- **No wordplay, no puns, no references** to anything outside the store.

## Rules for `action:`

The `action` field is the only place a writer's prose reaches the model, so it
is written for a machine, not a reader.

- Physical verbs and nameable objects only.
- No interiority — "she realizes" renders as nothing.
- No adjectives of mood. Mood is in [[Visual-System]], applied automatically.
- One event per clip. Ten seconds holds one thing happening.

## The failure mode

Writing that is *about* being tired at 3am instead of writing that is tired at
3am. If a beat needs the audience to notice how clever it is, it belongs to a
different brand.
